The arithmetic behind each number

Everything on this page falls out of four inputs. Change one and follow it through.

effective focal length = telescope focal length × barlow magnification = effective focal length ÷ eyepiece focal length true field = eyepiece apparent field ÷ magnification exit pupil = aperture ÷ magnification focal ratio = effective focal length ÷ aperture Dawes limit (arcsec)= 116 ÷ aperture in mm useful magnification= aperture ÷ 7 …up to… aperture × 2

The trade you can't escape: a shorter eyepiece raises magnification, which shrinks the true field and shrinks the exit pupil — so the image gets bigger, narrower and dimmer all at once. A wider apparent field buys back field without giving up magnification, which is the whole reason wide-angle eyepieces cost what they do.

Aperture does something the eyepiece can't. Swap eyepieces all day and the resolving limit never moves — only aperture changes it. Watch the star field thin out as you shrink the aperture: fewer stars are bright enough to reach you, and a close pair merges into one blur the moment the Dawes limit grows past its separation — try ε Lyrae at 40 mm, then at 150 mm.

The light path

—

Swipe the diagram sideways to follow the light all the way to the eye.

Light converges to the focal plane and opens out again at the same angle, so the beam reaching the eyepiece is the mirror of the one that left the objective — and its width there is the exit pupil, which is why the exit pupil equals eyepiece focal length divided by focal ratio. Your eye goes at that exit pupil, and the whole illuminated field of the eyepiece subtends the apparent field angle there. Lengths are compressed to fit and the apparent-field wedge is indicative; the cone angle really is the focal ratio, and every number is exact. What fits in this field

—

A caveat worth knowing. True field here is the classic AFOV ÷ magnification estimate. The exact answer uses the eyepiece's field stop diameter, which manufacturers don't always publish; the approximation runs a few percent optimistic on wide-angle eyepieces.

Angular sizes are real: the Moon 31′, Jupiter's disc 40″, Saturn's rings 44″ tip to tip, M31 nearly 3° end to end. Star fields are representative, not a survey catalogue. Never point a telescope at the Sun.
